## 0. The deliverable: a one-page PDF

**The final outcome of this project is a single-page PDF** that can be attached to an application
to Go Magic. Everything else in this repo exists to back it up.

It has to do four jobs on one side of A4, in this order of importance:

1. **Say the useful thing first.** The recommendation is *"for a one-off backfill over your
   existing log, fit jointly, not online — it is a 61% cut in error for nothing but compute"*.
   That is concrete, cheap, and immediately actionable, and it should be legible in ten seconds.
2. **Show the one chart.** `out/recovery.png` is the argument: how much data before a difficulty
   label means anything, and what gating costs. One figure, not three.
3. **Be honest about what it is not.** It does not claim their labels are wrong; it cannot,
   without their attempt log. Stating that unprompted is the point — it demonstrates knowing the
   difference between what a simulation can and cannot settle, which is the actual skill on show.
4. **Make the next step obvious and small.** Not "let me rebuild your difficulty system" but
   "run this over one month of your attempt log and see whether the numbers hold".

Constraints and cautions:

- **One page.** If it needs two, the argument is not finished.
- **No fabricated Go Magic branding, no invented data.** Every number is from this repo, and every
  claim about Go Magic is either sourced from the public page snapshot or explicitly flagged as
  unverified (see the sourcing table in `docs/METHOD.md`).
- Link to the repo and to `docs/METHOD.md` for the depth; the PDF is a door, not the room.
- The tone that fits: a colleague showing something interesting they found, not a candidate
  performing. The work is strong enough that overselling it would make it weaker.

**Status: a first draft exists** — `out/onepager.pdf`, generated by `docs/onepager.py`, which
hard-fails if the layout spills onto a second page. CI builds it (`--html-only`, since the PDF step
needs a browser). What remains is judgement, not code: whether the recommendation is the right one
to lead with, and whether the rank-scale caveat belongs on the page or only in the repo.

## 1. Corrections and open questions raised in review

### Done

- [x] **State the rating scale explicitly.** The old text was internally inconsistent — it claimed
      100 points per rank *and* "20k ≈ 700, 1d ≈ 2100", which implies 70. Now one convention
      (100 pts/rank, 1d = 2100), rendered by `as_rank()` and printed by `describe_scale()` at the
      top of every run.
- [x] **Confidence intervals.** Every table is now 10 repeats with a 95% t interval, and every
      regime contrast is *paired* on the same planted worlds. All contrasts are significant.
- [x] **Uneven traffic.** `--funnel` models a prerequisite tree's drop-off. It costs 40–50 RMSE
      points at matched total volume and damages the ordering under gating (ρ 0.94 → 0.78). See
      [`docs/FINDINGS.md`](docs/FINDINGS.md) section 7.
- [x] **The extended-sweep trap.** Running past 160 attempts appears to show gating stops
      mattering; it is the band fallback silently ungating the simulation. Documented, with the
      population-scaled control that refutes it.

### Open

- [ ] **The planted population is not Go Magic's.** ~10% of planted puzzles land above 1d, nothing
      lands below 20k, and the linear rank map is least accurate at high kyu where the tree's first
      tier sits. Fixing this properly needs their difficulty and player-strength distributions;
      short of that, a sensitivity run over two or three plausible population shapes would at least
      bound the effect.
- [ ] **Decide whether to restate the tables on the measured rank curve.** `docs/RESEARCH.md` §1 is
      no longer a survey of competing conventions — it is a measurement against 675,451 EGD games,
      and it settles the size of the effect: one rank is worth ~40 rating points through the kyu
      range and 96 at 1d, so "±100 ≈ one rank" is right at dan level and ~2.5× too generous at 10k.
      `docs/FINDINGS.md` now carries that curve. What is *not* decided: whether the planted
      population and every published table should be **restated** on it, and what the 2.36×
      compression figure becomes in those units. This is the open scale question, and it changes
      how every result reads in ranks — though not the results themselves.
      Two things make the decision harder rather than easier now that it is measured: the curve is
      only trustworthy down to ~12 kyu, and Go Magic's first tier sits below that; and the measured
      kyu-range figure (~40) is flatter than any published model predicts (49–72), because it is
      taken in noisy grade-label space rather than rating space. Restating on a curve that is
      itself extrapolated where it matters most may be worse than declaring the nominal map and its
      error, which is what the docs do today.
- [ ] **Hint damping is implemented but unused in the sweep.** `play(weight=…)` exists and is
      tested; the sweep runs undamped, so the gated regime is modelled slightly optimistically.
      Lichess's version is asymmetric (hinted win 0.2, hinted loss 0.7) and `_lerp` takes one
      symmetric weight. Worth a run with damping on before quoting the gated numbers as a floor.
- [ ] **RD is not a safe readiness gate on gated data** (`docs/METHOD.md` section 7): it reports
      ~77 points of uncertainty while making ~287 points of error. The obvious product move —
      "ship the label when RD drops below X" — is therefore unsafe as stated, and the repo should
      propose the calibrated alternative rather than leaving a warning. Needs: calibrate RD against
      realised error on anchor items, or gate on the ungated instrument's RD instead.

## 2. Research questions, and where the answers landed

Five questions came out of review. The literature sweep is
[`docs/RESEARCH.md`](docs/RESEARCH.md) — read its two caveat sections before acting on any of it.
Answers in one line, then the work each implies.

| question | answer |
| --- | --- |
| **Cold start** | Co-estimate with an informative prior; **no waiting period**. Glicko's `g(φ)` already downweights uncertain opponents (RD 350 contributes 0.45× the information of RD 45). Hard-exclude only on the *puzzle* side, as Lichess does at RD ≥ 230. |
| **Per-skill ratings** | One global rating **plus shrunk per-tag offsets** — never independent per-tag pools. Lichess ships exactly one puzzle rating and renders per-theme numbers as a gated descriptive breakdown. |
| **Time to solve** | Usable, but as a **scale anchor and hygiene filter**, not an accuracy fix. Budget 10–30%, not 2–3×. Requires per-attempt timestamps, which Go Magic may not have. |
| **PvP ratings** | **Same system, one pool.** Glicko-2 over Elo; WHR (Coulom) is the batch analogue and was designed for Go. OGS has run Go on Glicko-2 since 2017. |
| **Blending disciplines** | **Do not average separately-shrunk components** — that over-regresses the aggregate. Fit the overall directly from all attempts; blend only for *display*. |

### The work each implies

- [ ] **Seed new players from Go Diagnostics instead of 1500/RD 350.** The highest-value item on
      this list: they already own the instrument and are not using it as a prior. A 15–20 item
      diagnostic gives RD ≈ 78–90, worth ~15 well-targeted attempts of information for free.
- [ ] **Use the existing 11-level hand labels as the item-side prior.** Raised by the completeness
      critic and absent from the brief: it reframes the hand table from "the thing to replace" to
      "the prior to shrink away from as traffic accrues", which is exactly what the starved tail in
      section 7 needs. A one-line change to `batch_fit.py`'s `l2` term, and cheap to test.
- [ ] **Implement and *measure* Lichess's asymmetric cold-start rule** (skip the puzzle update when
      the player's RD ≥ 230; damp the player update when the puzzle is provisional). Either result
      is worth reporting — "we tested it and it moved the number by X" beats restating the rule.
- [ ] **Check the tag-hinting split.** Lichess damps rating updates when a theme name leaks the
      solution, and its `nonHintingThemes` set is precisely the *game-phase* tags. Go Magic's grid
      splits the same way: `{opening, middle-game, endgame}` is non-hinting, but
      `{fighting, tesuji, life-and-death, analysis, knowledge}` names the motif and hints hard.
      That maps one-to-one onto `play(weight=…)`, which is implemented, tested, and unused.
- [ ] **Re-check every per-tag recommendation under funnelled traffic.** The break-even bars
      (Feinberg–Wainer, Sinharay's ≥20 items) assume volume the funnel says most tags will not have.

### From the field: two *All Things Go* interviews (done)

Analysed in `docs/RESEARCH.md`, field-evidence section. Nothing contradicted a finding; three
things are new work.

- [ ] **Use OGS's format-average paradox as the argument against per-tag display.** OGS averages
      per-format ratings weighted by traffic, and the result is that you can win a game and lose
      rating. That is §5's shrinkage recommendation failing in production, in Go — a far better
      argument for a Go audience than James–Stein, and it belongs in the write-up.
- [ ] **Model effort contamination, or at least name it as a threat.** Low-effort attempts violate
      the clean-logistic assumption the whole simulation rests on. The cheapest version: a
      `--careless` knob that makes some fraction of attempts coin flips, to bound how much it
      moves the recovery curve.
- [ ] **Rapid improvers bias the items they touch.** Players improve between attempts; puzzles do
      not. `METHOD.md` §3 covers the drift asymmetry in one direction only. An improving player's
      early attempts push puzzle difficulties in a direction nothing later corrects.

### Not researched, and should have been

Both raised by the completeness critic; both are in-domain and likely to be the first things a Go
company asks about.

- [x] **Does any Go site already rate puzzles by attempts?** **Yes — since ~1999.** goproblems.com
      (Elo, per-problem K decaying ~127→10), Tsumego Hero (EGF GoR, both sides per attempt),
      probably 101weiqi. OGS does *not*, but has had an open issue for it since Feb 2022 while
      already recording the attempt counts. Full survey and licence audit in
      [`docs/PROBLEM-SOURCES.md`](docs/PROBLEM-SOURCES.md); `docs/FINDINGS.md` §1b now says this up front
      rather than implying novelty.
- [ ] **Engine-derived difficulty.** KataGo features — solution depth, branching factor, policy
      entropy, value swing at the key move — are the only thing that cold-starts all 10,160
      puzzles with *zero* traffic, which is exactly the failure mode section 7 identifies.
      Surveyed in `docs/PROBLEM-SOURCES.md`; not yet built.

### Collection work, now that the sources are known

- [ ] **Validate the whole method against Lichess's CC0 puzzle database.** 6.1M puzzles with real
      crowd-derived Glicko-2 ratings, deviations and play counts — the only such dataset in
      existence. It is chess, so it can never be Go content, but it is the one place the recovery
      curve can be checked against reality instead of against a planted world. This is the single
      highest-value item left in the repo.
- [ ] **Build the phase classifier properly.** ~92% of the world's tagged problems arrive with the
      phase axis blank. `build_collection.py` ships a stone-count stub that puts 421 of Go Game
      Guru's 422 problems in middle-game — honest, but not useful. Board coverage, corner/side/
      centre contact and settledness are the features.
- [ ] **Decide the knowledge-vs-life-and-death split** for the ~20 named group statuses
      (carpenter's square, bulky five, bent four…). Recall → knowledge, reading → life-and-death.
      No external site makes this distinction and it reassigns thousands of problems.
- [ ] **Author the opening row in-house.** Free material for `opening × {fighting, tesuji,
      life-and-death}` does not exist, and `opening × life-and-death` is empty for a structural
      reason: by move ~15 nothing is enclosed enough to be a tsumego. That is also where Go Magic's
      hand-authored catalogue is most differentiated.

## 3. Code follow-ups

- [ ] `docs/METHOD.md`'s "online Rasch" third-arm table is an ad-hoc 2-repeat run and is the only
      table in the repo not re-baselined at 10 repeats. Either commit the script that produced it
      or drop the arm.
- [ ] Consider a rating clamp (`[400, 4000]`, as lila has). It bounds the maximum gap below the
      ~6,447 points where the expected score saturates, which makes the saturation branch in
      `update()` unreachable — a cheaper defence than the limit it currently takes. **Now has a
      Go-specific motivation**: an OGS handicap exploit reportedly drove a test account to ~13 dan
      rating points in about ten games (`docs/RESEARCH.md`, field-evidence section).
- [ ] `--funnel` uses an exponential decay in difficulty percentile because it is the simplest
      one-parameter family with the right shape. If the real drop-off between tree nodes is ever
      known, replace it with that.
